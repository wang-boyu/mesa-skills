# Protocol and attribution

ODD describes a model in seven elements. “Overview,” “Design concepts,” and
“Details” explain the grouping; they do not replace the numbered elements.
Use the seven headings in the [template](../assets/ODD.md), with subordinate
headings as useful. Supporting bibliography or evidence notes can follow them.
ODD is a model description, not an experiment report or proof of validity.

A full document should identify the protocol and credit its origin and update.
For example: “This model description follows the ODD protocol introduced by
Grimm et al. (2006), using its 2020 second update (Grimm et al. 2020).” Include
the corresponding bibliography or links so the attribution travels with the
document. Preserve existing model authorship and source credit separately.

Under Design concepts, consider basic principles, emergence, adaptation,
objectives, learning, prediction, sensing, interaction, stochasticity,
collectives, and observation. Explain the concepts that apply; irrelevant ones
can be omitted. Distinguish imposed behavior from emergent outcomes. Describe
how observables are measured, including aggregation and timing.

Explain why consequential rules were chosen where the evidence supports that
rationale. A plausible rationale inferred from code is still an inference.
Available evaluation evidence may be cited with its limits, without creating
new results. Static datasets used only at time zero belong in Initialization;
Input data covers exogenous inputs varying during execution.

## Material human decisions

ODD+D adds useful questions about human decision-making. When such decisions
are material, state which component motivates its use and cite Müller et al.
(2013). Address applicable theoretical and empirical background, individual
decisions, objectives, sensing and prediction, interaction, adaptation and
learning, heterogeneity, and implementation details.

This skill integrates those concerns into the ODD 2020 elements, especially
Design concepts and Submodels. That integration is a practical composition of
the protocols, not a claim that the 2013 paper prescribed the later 2020
structure. Do not create parallel documents or empty decision sections.
Non-human agents can adapt or learn without representing human decisions;
geography alone does not activate this extension.

## Primary references

- Grimm et al. (2006), [A standard protocol for describing individual-based
  and agent-based models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2006.04.023),
  *Ecological Modelling* 198, 115–126.
- Grimm et al. (2020), [The ODD Protocol for Describing Agent-Based and Other
  Simulation Models: A Second Update to Improve Clarity, Replication, and
  Structural Realism](https://www.jasss.org/23/2/7.html), *JASSS* 23(2), 7,
  DOI [10.18564/jasss.4259](https://doi.org/10.18564/jasss.4259).

  for deeper element-level questions.
- Müller et al. (2013), [Describing human decisions in agent-based models -
  ODD+D, an extension of the ODD protocol](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2013.06.003),
  *Environmental Modelling & Software* 48, 37–48.
